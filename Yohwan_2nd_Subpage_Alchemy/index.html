Concord Review · Humanities Research

The Mirage of the Middle East

The purple haze is a visual representation of my Concord Review paper — how the colloquial term “Middle East” and the meaning of Orientalism have shifted across human history.

Through three case studies — from the Greco-Persian Era (500 BCE), to the Ilkhanate Era (13th century), to the modern tourism industry advertised by the Gulf States — the paper traces how perception and reality diverge, and how those mirages are remade over time.

This research grew from something that once seemed trivial: my time in Dubai, often spent fondling with clay. That tactile curiosity became a bridge into Chemistry — resembling the mechanism of Alchemy itself — while heritage sites and museums I visited, even without fully noting every historic detail, unconsciously shaped the direction of this inquiry into Orientalism.

The Meaning of the Gold

Gold symbolizes discovering my interest in Chemistry.

Gold is the visual representation of how seemingly useless clay inspired my main passion — my life-driven force. What began as a trivial material became the spark that led me into Chemistry, and Pioneer Academics Research is the physical product of that genuine commitment to the field.

The review asks: How can the molecular characteristics of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) biomarkers inform the rational design of biosensing technologies for early cancer detection?

Thesis

Current ctDNA research has largely emphasized discovering new biomarkers and improving sensing technologies independently. However, the rational design of next-generation biosensors should begin with the molecular chemistry of the biomarker itself, because biomarker properties fundamentally determine the requirements for molecular recognition and analytical detection.
