Musical Journey

2019

Experience in Dubai

Turning point where violin shifted from a forced task by my parents to an active hobby that defined me and connected me to international culture at Regents. I was given the opportunity to play for the Emirates Youth Symphony Orchestra where I met the Sheikh of Dubai, Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum.

2022

Continuing my ABRSM journey

Violin was no longer just an instrument I played, but a medium that shaped my life and friendships across the globe. Coming back to Korea, a cultural shift happened again, but violin again played a role of bridging the gap between me and the students who previously had been attending Seoul Academy. ABRSM was the visible marker that I continued from my time in Dubai until I left Korea to head to St. Andrew’s.

2023–2024

String Quartet & Orchestra

Violin opened up different paths for me as I stepped into the St. Andrew’s Campus. The first time when I went to the orchestra practice changed my previous perception of the ensemble. Going from a violin accompanist in Dubai to a small band-like orchestra in Seoul Academy (Korea), St. Andrew’s orchestra looked like what I was dreaming of: an inclusive space where individual talents turn into a space of learning and harmony. The concert master of my orchestra was no longer someone who simply wanted the title for their selfish benefit, but an overqualified leader who was competent to lead and improve the orchestra. Especially the winter concert featuring the famous waltz and sugar plum fairy felt dream-like, changing my perspective on my violin as a musical instrument.

2025–2026

Violin and I, Today

I play violin till today. Continuing the legacy of Gabe the concert master and other Senior heads of their respective leading roles, I still perform as the Chamber Society and first violin of the orchestra. Most recently, for Awards Night at the Graduation Ceremony, I played as a Chamber Society. Although the role changed throughout my journey across different communities, there is no doubt that the violin is my steadfast best friend.

Music

Musical Resonance

While rehearsing a piece from Spirited Away in the chapel, I became captivated by the way each note lingered in the air. The sound seemed richer and more sustained than in our rehearsal room. Although I had experienced these differences in chapels, concert halls, and even gymnasiums, I had never stopped to ask why.

As I learned more about sound, my curiosity gradually shifted from where music was performed to the instrument producing it. This made me wonder why different woods create different tones and resonances.

Learning about material science revealed far more than why violins sound different. I was fascinated that properties invisible to the eye could dramatically shape what we hear. Understanding how density, stiffness, and internal structure influence resonance answered one question but opened a larger one: how do microscopic material properties shape macroscopic experiences?

Chemistry

Chemistry Resonance

Three woods of the violin — and how material science shapes the frequencies we hear.

Spruce — Soundboard

Light, stiff, and highly anisotropic. Spruce’s low density and strong grain direction let the top plate vibrate freely, converting string energy into airborne sound with remarkable efficiency.

  • Density Low — favors loud projection
  • Stiffness High along the grain
  • Resonance Broad, singing fundamentals

Ebony — Fingerboard

Extremely dense and hard. Ebony resists wear under the fingers and damps excess vibration so the neck stays stable while the body — not the fingerboard — carries the resonant voice.

  • Density Very high — durable contact surface
  • Stiffness Rigid under string pressure
  • Resonance Controlled damping, clean attack

Maple — Back & Ribs

Denser than spruce, maple reflects and shapes sound from within the body. Its figure and elastic properties help define projection, focus, and the violin’s characteristic warmth.

  • Density Medium-high — reflective shell
  • Stiffness Balanced elasticity
  • Resonance Focused overtones & body color
